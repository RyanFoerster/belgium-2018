Moscow, 23 June 2018. The eleven who start against Tunisia.

The one they were supposed to win.

Belgium had never finished higher than fourth at a World Cup. In June 2018 they were ranked third in the world, with a group of players who would not get many more summers like this one.

Roberto Martínez had the squad every Belgian coach before him could only imagine, and Thierry Henry beside him on the bench. They arrived in Russia unbeaten in qualifying: nine wins, one draw, forty-three goals. The first European side to book a ticket.

The talk at home was no longer about getting out of the group. It was about what a generation owes itself when it knows it will not get many more summers like this.

Fellaini and Chadli, the two substitutes who turned Rostov, both start. De Bruyne leaves midfield and plays as the central forward. Lukaku — a number nine all his life — goes out to the right, into the space behind Marcelo.

Thirteen minutes in, Chadli's corner, Kompany's flick, Fernandinho's arm: an own goal, and Belgium lead.
